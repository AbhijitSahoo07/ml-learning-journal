# Cooperative Game Theory

## Overview
Cooperative Game Theory is a branch of game theory that studies how rational players can cooperate to achieve a common goal, often by forming coalitions. Unlike non-cooperative game theory, which focuses on individual decision-making and competition, cooperative game theory assumes that players can make binding agreements and form groups (coalitions) to maximize their collective payoff. The central questions revolve around how the gains from cooperation should be distributed among the coalition members and what makes a coalition stable.

## What Problem It Solves
Cooperative Game Theory primarily addresses problems related to:
1.  **Fair Distribution of Gains/Costs:** How to equitably share the benefits generated by a group effort or the costs incurred by a joint project among the participants.
2.  **Incentivizing Cooperation:** Designing mechanisms that encourage individuals to join and contribute to a coalition rather than acting alone.
3.  **Coalition Formation and Stability:** Predicting which groups of players are likely to form and remain stable, given the potential gains and individual contributions.
4.  **Resource Allocation:** Optimizing the allocation of shared resources or responsibilities among cooperating entities to achieve collective efficiency.

## How It Works
In Cooperative Game Theory, the focus shifts from individual strategies to the value that different groups (coalitions) of players can generate.
1.  **Players and Coalitions:** A set of players $N$ exists. Any subset of $N$ is called a coalition $S$.
2.  **Characteristic Function:** A key concept is the "characteristic function," denoted as $v(S)$. This function assigns a numerical value to each possible coalition $S$, representing the maximum payoff that coalition $S$ can guarantee for itself, regardless of what players outside $S$ do. This value is often interpreted as the "worth" or "strength" of the coalition.
3.  **Binding Agreements:** It's assumed that players can communicate, negotiate, and enforce binding agreements. This means once a coalition forms and agrees on a distribution, they stick to it.
4.  **Solution Concepts:** The theory then proposes "solution concepts" to determine how the total value generated by the grand coalition (all players cooperating) should be distributed among its members. Popular solution concepts include:
    *   **The Core:** A set of payoff distributions where no sub-coalition has an incentive to break away and achieve a better outcome for its members on their own.
    *   **The Shapley Value:** A unique distribution that fairly allocates the total gain based on each player's average marginal contribution to all possible coalitions.

## Mathematical Intuition
Let $N = \{1, 2, \dots, n\}$ be the set of players.
A **coalition** is any subset $S \subseteq N$.
The **characteristic function** $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}$ assigns a value to each coalition, representing the maximum total payoff its members can achieve together.
$v(\emptyset) = 0$ (the empty coalition achieves nothing).

An **imputation** is a vector $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ representing a distribution of the total value $v(N)$ among the players, such that:
1.  **Individual Rationality:** $x_i \ge v(\{i\})$ for all $i \in N$ (no player gets less than what they could achieve alone).
2.  **Collective Rationality:** $\sum_{i \in N} x_i = v(N)$ (the entire value of the grand coalition is distributed).

The **Core** is the set of all imputations $x$ such that for every coalition $S \subseteq N$:
$$ \sum_{i \in S} x_i \ge v(S) $$
This means no coalition $S$ can improve its members' payoffs by defecting from the grand coalition and forming $S$ on its own. If the core is empty, it implies instability.

The **Shapley Value** $\phi_i(v)$ for player $i$ is the average of player $i$'s marginal contributions to all possible coalitions. It is calculated as:
$$ \phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(n - |S| - 1)!}{n!} (v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)) $$
where $n$ is the total number of players, $|S|$ is the size of coalition $S$, and $(v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S))$ is player $i$'s marginal contribution to coalition $S$. The Shapley value is unique and satisfies properties like symmetry, dummy player, and additivity.

## Advantages
*   **Fairness and Equity:** Provides principled ways (like Shapley Value) to distribute gains or costs fairly among participants.
*   **Stability Analysis:** Helps identify stable agreements (e.g., using the Core) that are less prone to defection.
*   **Resource Optimization:** Facilitates efficient allocation of resources or tasks in collaborative settings.
*   **Understanding Power Dynamics:** Reveals the relative importance or contribution of each player to the collective outcome.
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Offers a framework for resolving disputes over resource sharing or benefit distribution.

## Disadvantages
*   **Complexity:** Can become computationally intensive for a large number of players, especially when calculating the Shapley Value (requires evaluating $2^n$ coalitions).
*   **Assumption of Binding Agreements:** Relies on the strong assumption that agreements are perfectly enforceable, which may not hold in real-world scenarios.
*   **Defining the Characteristic Function:** It can be challenging to accurately determine the value $v(S)$ for every possible coalition, especially in complex situations.
*   **Empty Core:** The Core might be empty, indicating that no stable distribution exists, which doesn't provide a clear solution.
*   **Lack of Process:** Focuses on the outcome (distribution) rather than the negotiation process itself.

## Real World Applications
1.  **Resource Sharing in Networks:** In telecommunications or cloud computing, multiple users or organizations might share a common resource (e.g., bandwidth, server capacity). Cooperative game theory can determine fair pricing or allocation schemes that incentivize efficient use and ensure no participant feels exploited.
2.  **Joint Ventures and Mergers:** When companies merge or form a joint venture, cooperative game theory can help determine how the combined profits or costs should be distributed among the participating entities, considering their individual contributions and bargaining power.
3.  **Cost Allocation in Public Goods:** For projects like building a bridge, maintaining a shared infrastructure, or funding a public service, cooperative game theory can be used to fairly allocate the costs among the beneficiaries (e.g., different cities or departments) based on their usage or benefit derived.

## Python Example
This example demonstrates the calculation of the Shapley Value for a simple 3-player game. We'll manually define the characteristic function and then compute each player's Shapley value.

```python
import itertools

def calculate_shapley_value(num_players, characteristic_function):
    """
    Calculates the Shapley Value for each player in a cooperative game.

    Args:
        num_players (int): The total number of players.
        characteristic_function (dict): A dictionary where keys are tuples
                                        representing coalitions (e.g., (0, 1) for players 0 and 1)
                                        and values are the worth of that coalition.
                                        The empty coalition must be defined as v(()) = 0.

    Returns:
        list: A list of Shapley values for each player.
    """
    shapley_values = [0.0] * num_players
    players = range(num_players)

    for i in players:
        player_i_shapley = 0.0
        # Iterate over all possible coalitions S that do NOT contain player i
        for k in range(num_players): # k is the size of the coalition S
            for S_tuple in itertools.combinations([p for p in players if p != i], k):
                S = set(S_tuple)
                S_with_i = tuple(sorted(list(S | {i}))) # Coalition S U {i}
                S_without_i = tuple(sorted(list(S)))    # Coalition S

                # Get values from the characteristic function
                v_S_with_i = characteristic_function.get(S_with_i, 0)
                v_S_without_i = characteristic_function.get(S_without_i, 0)

                # Calculate marginal contribution
                marginal_contribution = v_S_with_i - v_S_without_i

                # Calculate weight: |S|! * (n - |S| - 1)! / n!
                # Using math.factorial for this
                import math
                weight = (math.factorial(len(S)) * math.factorial(num_players - len(S) - 1)) / math.factorial(num_players)

                player_i_shapley += weight * marginal_contribution
        shapley_values[i] = player_i_shapley
    return shapley_values

# --- Define a 3-player game ---
# Players are 0, 1, 2
# Characteristic function v(S)
# Keys are sorted tuples representing coalitions
game_v = {
    (): 0,          # Empty coalition
    (0,): 10,       # Player 0 alone
    (1,): 20,       # Player 1 alone
    (2,): 5,        # Player 2 alone
    (0, 1): 40,     # Players 0 and 1 together (synergy: 40 > 10+20)
    (0, 2): 20,     # Players 0 and 2 together
    (1, 2): 30,     # Players 1 and 2 together
    (0, 1, 2): 60   # All players together
}

num_players = 3
shapley_values = calculate_shapley_value(num_players, game_v)

print(f"Shapley Values for {num_players} players:")
for i, val in enumerate(shapley_values):
    print(f"Player {i}: {val:.2f}")

# Expected output (approximate):
# Player 0: 21.67
# Player 1: 31.67
# Player 2: 6.67
# Sum: 60.00 (which is v((0,1,2)))
```

## Interview Questions
1.  **What is the fundamental difference between Cooperative and Non-Cooperative Game Theory?**
    *   **Answer:** Cooperative Game Theory focuses on how players can form binding agreements and coalitions to maximize collective payoffs, then distribute those gains. Non-Cooperative Game Theory, conversely, analyzes individual decision-making where players act independently to maximize their own utility, without the ability to form binding agreements, often leading to competitive outcomes.

2.  **Explain the concepts of the "Core" and "Shapley Value" in Cooperative Game Theory.**
    *   **Answer:** The **Core** is a set of payoff distributions for the grand coalition such that no sub-coalition has an incentive to break away and achieve a better outcome for its members independently. If a distribution is in the Core, it's stable against defection. The **Shapley Value** is a unique solution concept that assigns a fair share of the total gain to each player based on their average marginal contribution to all possible coalitions. It provides a specific, equitable distribution, unlike the Core which can be a set of distributions or even empty.

3.  **What is the characteristic function in Cooperative Game Theory, and why is it important?**
    *   **Answer:** The characteristic function, denoted $v(S)$, assigns a numerical value to every possible coalition $S$ of players. This value represents the maximum payoff or "worth" that coalition $S$ can guarantee for itself, regardless of the actions of players outside $S$. It's crucial because it quantifies the potential gains from cooperation for any group, forming the basis for calculating fair distributions and analyzing coalition stability.
